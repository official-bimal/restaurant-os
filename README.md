# Restaurant OS

Orders, kitchen display and billing for restaurants and cafes (Nepal-first). Separate from
`customer_os` (CinePaila): own code, own database. Stack mirrors it: Next.js 16, TypeScript,
Prisma 7, PostgreSQL, Tailwind 4, Zod, Vitest.

Research behind the design: `../research/restrox/`.

## Run locally

```bash
npm install
cp .env.example .env        # then set SESSION_SECRET (and DATABASE_URL for a real Postgres)
npm run db:dev              # embedded dev database on :5433 (leave running, own terminal)
npx prisma db push          # apply schema to it (first time)
npm run db:seed             # demo cafe: owner@demo.test (password in prisma/seed.ts)
npm run dev                 # http://localhost:3000
```

For the embedded database set `DB_POOL_MAX=1` and
`DATABASE_URL="postgresql://postgres:postgres@127.0.0.1:5433/postgres"`. It serves one
connection at a time, so `psql`/scripts cannot connect while the app holds it. Use a real
PostgreSQL for anything beyond local development (`npm run db:deploy` applies migrations).

## Sign-in protection

Sign-ins are rate limited in the database: 5 failed attempts per email per network per 15
minutes, 30 per network, and 50 per email per hour. Set `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS` to the number of
proxies in front of the app (1 on most hosts) so the network is identified correctly; with the
default 0 the network counter is skipped and the per-email limit (5 per 15 minutes) applies to
everyone, which means a stranger can lock an account for 15 minutes. Changing a password also
limits wrong "current password" guesses (5 per 15 minutes).

## Checks

`npm run typecheck` · `npm run lint` · `npm test` (unit) · `npm run test:integration` (needs `db:dev`)

## What exists (Phase 1, in progress)

Notifications (one list of guest orders and requests, low stock, low ratings and bills left open from an earlier day, built live from existing data) · add-ons and extras (size and extras groups per dish, required or optional, priced, copied onto the order line and shown on the kitchen ticket; staff-only, guests get the plain dish and cannot order dishes with a required choice) · menu sets and a weekly schedule (Breakfast, Lunch: only the active set can be ordered, by staff and guests; pick one by hand or follow the schedule; no set means the whole menu) · delivery orders (name, phone, address, rider; preparing, out, delivered or failed; paid like any bill) · Multi-branch (owner adds and switches off branches; each branch has its own areas, tables, orders, invoices and day close; a header switcher for people with more than one branch; staff can be limited to chosen branches; the menu, recipes, customers, loyalty and suppliers stay shared by the whole business; stock is per branch (see below); every service filters by business and branch) · Login (signed session cookie, argon2) · roles (owner, manager, cashier, waiter, kitchen) ·
menu management (categories, dishes, stations, availability) and table management (areas, tables) for owner and manager · staff management (add with a one-time temporary password, change role, reset password, remove) and a self-service password page · move a party to another table, merge two bills, split a bill by items (each part paid and invoiced separately) and an even-split guide · purchase orders and suppliers (reorder suggestions from low stock, an order text to send yourself, part and full deliveries that update stock and average cost, price variance; the app does not contact suppliers) · campaigns (customer segments, prepared messages for staff to send themselves, consent re-checked every time, win-back bonus points with attribution; the app never sends messages) · stock and recipes (stock items in g/ml/pieces, purchases with average cost, waste, counts, recipes with food cost and margin, automatic use when a bill is paid, low-stock warning, food cost in the daily report) · guest feedback (one-tap star rating after paying, private comment, the public review link shown to every rater, low ratings flagged to the manager until handled) · daily sales report in the business's own time zone and day close with cash reconciliation (closing locks the day; only the owner can reopen) · cancel an order (with a reason) and refund a paid invoice in full with a credit note and points reversal, plus a Bills list · customers and automatic loyalty points (a guest adds their phone on the QR page or staff add it at the counter; points are earned on every paid bill and spent in blocks at checkout; customer list, adjustments, ledger, erase) · QR table ordering (guest scans the table QR, builds a cart, staff accept it; call waiter, water, request bill; sitting ends at checkout; printable QR posters; reset-QR) · orders for dine-in and takeaway with idempotent creation · items with status flow and
cancel-with-reason · kitchen display grouped by station with ticket ageing · bill with
discount, service charge, VAT, rounding, split payments, sequential invoices · audit log.

Not built yet: phone verification (OTP) and guest-visible balances, pay-from-phone, Nepali language, password reset by email, silent printing straight to a receipt printer, and live updates for the public guest and tracking pages (they still poll every 5-8 s).

Stock by branch: the stock items (name, unit) and recipes are shared, but each branch keeps its own quantity, reorder level, average cost and history; a sale takes stock from the branch that made it, a purchase order is for one branch and its delivery goes into that branch, low-stock alerts and the Notifications list are per branch, and a branch added later starts with every item at zero. Existing stock was assigned to the first branch when this was added. **Transfers** move stock from the branch you are in to another: it leaves the sender at once and stays **in transit** until the receiving branch books it in (counting what actually arrived; any shortfall is recorded as lost on the way) or it is sent back; tick "arrives straight away" to skip the in-transit step. You cannot send more than the branch holds; it travels at the sender's average cost, folded into the receiver's on arrival; the receiving branch's stock people get a push alert and a notification. **All branches and transfers** (under Stock) shows every branch's levels side by side (with what is on the way) for the branches a person works in. An item can be switched off for one branch (**Not stocked at this branch**: hidden from its lists, alerts, orders and sales deductions; it must be at zero there and nothing on its way). **Value and cost** (owners and managers) shows stock value per branch and item at each branch's average cost, what is in transit, and cost of sales, purchases, waste, counts, transfers and losses for any period.

Newer features, in more detail: delivery zones per branch with a fee added to the bill after tax (no service charge or VAT on the fee; loyalty points earned on the food only), a customer tracking link (`/track/<token>`: progress and the rider's first name, never the phone, address or prices) and a rider link (`/rider/<token>`: address, phone, cash to collect, picked up / delivered / could not deliver). Both links are unguessable per-order tokens that anyone holding them can open, so share each only with its person. Guests can pick add-on choices on their phone (priced on the server, checked again when staff accept); add-ons can use stock, taken off when the bill is paid. Menu sets are shared but the active set and the weekly schedule are per branch. Notifications can be marked read (per person), with an optional chime and desktop pop-ups while the app is open in a browser tab.

Delivery pricing can be by distance (bands such as up to 2 km, up to 5 km; the waiter enters the distance from a map app, and beyond the last band is refused) or by flat-fee zone, per branch; VAT on the delivery fee is a setting. Riders can have their own accounts (role Rider): a rider signs in to a page that shows only the deliveries assigned to them, can call the customer, and taps picked up, delivered or could not deliver; they can share their location while a delivery is out (kept only as the latest point, shown to staff and the customer as a link to a map, and wiped when the delivery ends). The rider link still works for riders without accounts. Background alerts use Web Push: each person turns them on for their own device; they carry only a short line such as "Table T3 sent an order" and reach the right staff (floor staff of the table's branch for guest orders and requests, managers for low ratings, the rider for a new delivery). Set `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`, `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` and `VAPID_SUBJECT` (see `.env.example`); without them the alerts are simply off. Push needs https in production (localhost works for testing).

Delivery, continued. **Automatic distance:** set a branch location (coordinates or a map link) on the Branches page; when a waiter pastes the customer's location, the distance and fee are worked out for them (straight line stretched by a road factor, default 130%), or from your own OSRM routing server if you set `ROUTING_URL` (only coordinates are sent). Typed distance still works. **Live map:** the order page and the customer's tracking page draw the restaurant, the delivery address and the rider in true proportion on the page itself, with no map tiles and no outside service, and refresh by themselves. **Dispatch board** (`/dispatch`): deliveries by stage (needs a rider, assigned, on the road, finished today) with riders' workloads and one-tap assignment. **Rider cash and pay:** cash a rider collects for unpaid bills and pay earned per delivery (a rate per rider, copied onto each delivery when it ends) are tracked as an add-only account, with hand-ins and pay-outs that cannot exceed what is owed; riders see their own balance. **Installable app:** a web app manifest and icons let the app be added to the home screen, which is what iPhone needs for alerts (the toggle explains the steps). **More alerts:** a sale that pushes stock from fine to low notifies the people who see stock; `/api/cron/alerts` (set `CRON_SECRET`, call it from any scheduler) sends one push a day per branch about bills left open and one about stock that is still low.

**Delivery runs and routes.** On `/dispatch`, tick two or more deliveries still being prepared and **Make a run** (up to 12 stops, optionally with a rider). The stops are ordered by a nearest-first route that starts at the branch, then improved by swapping stops (a sensible order, not a guaranteed shortest one); the rider's page shows "Stop N of M". Sending the run out sends every delivery in it at once; a run can be edited or broken up until it leaves, and finishes when its last delivery ends. **Street address to location:** a place is remembered per phone number and reused only when the address words are the same (so a customer who moved is not sent to the old spot). With `GEOCODER_URL` set (a Nominatim-compatible server, optionally `GEOCODER_COUNTRY`) an address that has no remembered place is looked up; without it nothing is sent anywhere and staff paste a location as before. Only the address text goes to that service.

**Branch menu, fully.** On the Menu page's **This branch** tab a branch can set its own price and availability for a dish, hide a whole category, send a dish to a different kitchen station (the kitchen display at that branch follows it), and set its own extra price (or switch off) for an add-on choice; staff ordering, guest ordering and accepting a guest order all use the branch's values.

**All-branches sales report** (`/reports/branches`, owners and managers): each branch's own daily report for a range of days plus the combined total, which is the exact sum of the branches. A person sees only the branches they work in.

**Supplier bills** (`/stock/bills`, owner and manager): enter a supplier's invoice (optionally linked to a purchase order), record payments (cash, bank, QR, cheque), see what is owed and what is overdue by supplier and age. Payments are add-only, can never total more than the bill (checked under a lock), and a bill with payments cannot be voided. The app records payments you made; it does not move money.

**Staff time and payroll.** Everyone can clock in and out from the header; managers add or correct hours (no overlapping shifts, a reason is required to remove one, everything is audited). Owners set each person's pay (hourly or monthly, overtime after a daily threshold at a multiple of the rate), add allowances, bonuses, advances and deductions per month, preview the month and **finalize** it: that saves a copy of the figures (never recalculated) and locks that month's hours and adjustments; lines are marked paid once. **Pay shown is before tax and statutory deductions: this app does not calculate tax, social security or provident fund, and is not a statement of what the law requires you to pay or withhold.**

**Sending campaign messages.** Off by default. Set `MESSAGING_PROVIDER` to `twilio` (`TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID`, `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN`, `TWILIO_FROM`), `sparrow` (`SPARROW_TOKEN`, `SPARROW_FROM`) or `webhook` (`MESSAGING_WEBHOOK_URL`, optional `MESSAGING_WEBHOOK_TOKEN`; the app POSTs `{to, text}` as JSON) and, optionally, `MESSAGING_DAILY_LIMIT` (default 200 a day per business). Only the owner can send, and only from the campaign page, after confirming the exact number shown ("Yes, send N"); nothing is ever sent automatically. Each person's consent is re-checked at the moment of sending, a message is claimed in a log before the provider is called so nobody is texted twice for one campaign, failed ones stay queued for a retry, and phone numbers are stored masked in the log. **The three provider integrations follow each provider's published API but have only been tested here against fake responses, not live services: send one message to yourself first.** Without a provider, the manual "open in messages" flow still works.

**Offline billing and payments.** On **Quick order** a cashier can now take an order, work out the bill, take payment (cash, QR or card, or split between them, with an optional percent discount and an optional customer phone for loyalty points), and print a receipt, all with **no connection at all**. It is sent to the server when the connection returns. How the hard parts work:
- **Server-side totals.** While online, the phone fetches a *signed snapshot* from the server: the plain dishes this branch can sell with this branch's prices, the tax settings (service charge, VAT, rounding) and the tables, signed with the server's secret (HMAC). The phone works the bill out from the snapshot with the very same pure code the server uses (`src/features/offline/billing.ts`), so the total on the receipt is the total the server gets. When the bill arrives, the server **verifies the signature, prices every line from the snapshot (never from a price the phone sends), works the total out again and compares**; a bill whose total differs is refused and flagged, not recorded. A customer pays what the menu said when they paid: a later price change does not alter a bill made earlier. A snapshot more than 30 days old is not accepted for billing.
- **Unbroken invoice numbers.** Each phone is given a *block* of 50 consecutive invoice numbers, taken from the business's single counter at that moment, so a number can never be used twice (not by two phones, and not by a bill made online). The phone uses its block in order and tops up automatically when it runs low and is online. **Every number is either on an invoice or listed as a gap with a reason**: if a phone is lost or retired, the owner closes its block (Owner control > Offline invoice numbers) and the unused numbers are recorded as gaps; if that phone later does send a bill with one of those numbers, the gap is removed and the bill takes its place. The gaps are in the accountant's Excel file ("Number gaps"). **Limit:** bills made offline are numbered when the sale happens but reach the server later, so invoice numbers are not always in time order across phones. Check with your accountant that this suits the rules that apply to you; the app makes no claim about tax compliance.
- **Safe to repeat and safe to lose connection.** The bill is saved on the phone *before* it is shown as paid and is kept until the server confirms it; each bill carries its own key, so sending it twice records it once; a bill the server cannot accept (total mismatch, a number that was not reserved for that phone, a number already used, a day that has been closed) is **never half recorded**, stays on the phone as "needs a person" with the reason, and can be sent again. The invoice is dated when the sale really happened. If that day was already closed, the bill waits until the owner reopens the day.
- **What is not offline.** Delivery, dishes with required choices (size, spice), add-ons, spending loyalty points, split or merged bills and refunds need the server. QR and card payments are recorded as the cashier enters them; with no connection they cannot be checked until later. A phone clears its storage (or is replaced) at the cost of any bills not yet sent: the page warns and keeps the list, but the safest habit is to connect and let it send before the end of the day.
- **Receipts** print as a PDF built on the phone (same layout and paper size as the saved Printing settings), so they work with no connection. The offline receipt says "Offline sale" in place of the order number, because the server gives the order its number when the bill arrives.
- **Tested** with automated tests for the pricing, the number blocks, the signature and every refusal above, and in the browser with the connection switched off; **not yet tested on real phones** over a long offline period.

**Offline order taking** (`/quick`, "Quick order" in the menu). Open it once while online; it keeps the menu and tables on the phone and a small service worker keeps the page itself. If the Wi-Fi drops, dine-in and takeaway orders (items and notes) are saved on the phone and sent automatically when the connection returns; each carries its own key so a repeat send never creates a second order. If the server refuses something (a table now in use, a dish switched off, a different branch), the order is shown as "needs a person" with the reason instead of vanishing. **Limits:** bills, payments, discounts, delivery, add-ons and guest QR orders need the connection; the page only opens offline if it was opened online on that phone before; saved orders live in the browser's storage (clearing site data deletes unsent ones); prices shown offline are those from the last time the page loaded; a table that someone else took meanwhile will be refused at sync. This was tested through the server side (idempotent sync, refusals); the offline behaviour of the page itself in a phone browser has not been exercised on real devices.

**Partial refunds.** On a paid order, "Refund this invoice" takes an amount (leave it empty to refund everything that is left), a reason, and optionally one method to hand the money back by. An invoice can have several credit notes, each numbered in its own sequence; together they can never come to more than the invoice, checked under a lock so two people refunding at once cannot overshoot. Each note takes its proportional share of the subtotal, discount, service charge, VAT and round-off, and the note that finishes the refund takes exactly what is left, so the notes always add up to the invoice. Money goes back by the original methods in proportion (or by the one method chosen, if the bill was paid by enough of it); the customer's points are put back in the same proportion and the visit count drops only when the bill is fully refunded. Food already made is not returned to stock. Part refunds are by amount: there is no item picker that works out the amount for you. The Bills list shows "Part refunded".

**Printing.** Print buttons on the order page open a clean page sized for 80 mm (or 58 mm with `?w=58`) paper: **Print receipt** (items, add-ons, notes, discount, service charge, VAT, delivery fee, payments, refunds, the business's PAN if set), **Print** on each credit note (with a signature line) and **Print kitchen ticket** (live items grouped by station, following a branch's own station choice, with notes and add-ons). It uses the browser's normal print dialog and whatever printer the device has chosen; to skip the dialog on a shop tablet, start the browser in kiosk-printing mode. Direct thermal-printer drivers (ESC/POS over USB or network), automatic printing when an order is placed, and a logo are not built. Receipt and credit-note prints are written to the audit log; the receipt is a plain record of the sale and is **not** claimed to meet any tax authority's invoice format: check what your accountant needs.

**Live updates.** Signed-in pages (orders, order detail, kitchen, dispatch, dashboard, notifications, rider deliveries) now listen to a live channel (`/api/live`, server-sent events). When anyone changes something in the same business and branch, or a guest sends an order or a request, the open pages re-read themselves within a fraction of a second; only a topic name travels on the channel, the data is read the normal permission-checked way. A slow 30-second refresh stays as a safety net, and if the channel cannot connect the pages poll as before. The channel lives inside one server process: with several app servers behind a load balancer, changes reach only the pages connected to the same server (the 30-second refresh covers the rest), and a shared channel such as Redis or Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY would be needed for instant updates everywhere. Each person can hold at most 6 open streams. Behind a reverse proxy, turn response buffering off for `/api/live`.

**Dish ratings for staff.** The Feedback page now has a **Dish ratings** table: every dish guests rated in the last 30 days with its average, number of ratings and how many gave 1 to 5 stars, lowest average first. A dish rated 3.5 or lower by at least 3 guests is marked "look into it", and dishes with fewer than 3 ratings are marked "few ratings" (guests see a dish's stars on their menu only from 3 ratings). Ratings are anonymous: the table shows counts only, never which guest or bill. A dish removed from the menu keeps its past ratings under "Removed dish". The period is fixed at 30 days for now.

**Clock in at the cafe, alerts and attendance.** Under Time, an owner or manager can switch on "Staff must be at the cafe to clock in" and set how close counts (30 to 1000 m, default 150 m) for the branch they are in; the branch needs a location on the Branches page first. With it on, the Clock in button reads the phone's location once, at the moment of the tap, and refuses if the phone is too far, or not exact enough (over 150 m of uncertainty), or location is blocked. Only the distance from the cafe is stored, never coordinates. Clocking out is always allowed (so nobody is stuck on the clock), but it records and tells you when someone clocked out away from the cafe. Owners and managers get a push alert and a notification when staff clock in or out ("Sita clocked in at 08:57 · 12 m from the cafe"; "clocked out · worked 7h 30m"). **Attendance** (`/attendance`, owners and managers) lists for any day who is in now, who came and left (first in, last out, hours, away-at-clock-out flag) and who was off, with counts, and the dashboard shows a "Staff today" summary. **Limits:** phones share their location only over a secure (https) connection, so on a plain-http LAN address the location check cannot work and clocking in is refused with an explanation: set up https before switching the rule on. A phone can be made to report a false location, so this stops casual clock-ins from home but is not proof against a determined cheat. With no shift schedule, "off" simply means no time recorded that day.

**Phone-first staff app.** On phones the staff app now has a bottom tab bar with the four screens each role uses all day (owner and manager: Dashboard, Orders, Kitchen, Alerts; cashier and waiter: Orders, Quick order, Kitchen, Alerts; kitchen: Kitchen, Alerts, Stock) and a More drawer for everything else, an alert bell with a count in the top bar, a compact two-column dashboard, inputs sized for thumbs (and 16 px so iPhones do not zoom), tables that scroll sideways inside their card, and room for the notch and home bar. Wide screens keep the sidebar.

**Landing page** (`/`, public). A marketing page with the product's story: hero, features, the guest QR menu, the staff side, how it works. It contains no customer logos, testimonials, user counts or regulatory claims because there are none to make. The product name and tagline live in `src/lib/brand.ts` (the name is still the working name "Restaurant OS"); set `NEXT_PUBLIC_CONTACT_EMAIL` to show a "Contact us" button. Signed-in visitors see "Open your dashboard".

**Nepali on the guest pages.** Every guest page (the QR menu, cart, order status, call waiter, loyalty box, "thank you" messages and the rating screen) has an **English / नेपाली** switch at the top. The choice is remembered on the guest's phone, and the page starts in English and switches as soon as it loads. The words live in `src/features/guest/i18n.ts` (a test checks every text has both languages and the same placeholders); dish and category names use the optional Nepali names set on the Menu page. **Not translated:** error messages that come back from the server after an action (for example "Some dishes are no longer available"), the staff app, receipts and printed pages. The Nepali wording was written by an AI model, not a professional translator: have a Nepali speaker read it and tell me what to change.

**Printing: PDF, any paper size, printer files and a print queue.** Every Print button now opens a small menu: **PDF** (a real PDF at the paper size you choose, to open, save or print from anywhere), **Browser print** (the earlier page), **Printer file** (the raw bytes a thermal printer reads, ESC/POS) and **Text**. The paper size defaults to the business's saved setting and can be changed for just that print (width in mm; height empty = exactly as long as the slip). **Printing** (in the Admin menu, owners and managers) holds the defaults: paper width 40 to 300 mm, fixed or automatic height, margins, text size, a line under the business name, a footer line, whether to show the PAN, address and customer name, large text on kitchen slips, receipt copies, and a live preview. The receipt, credit note and kitchen slip all come from one layout, so the PDF, the printer file and the preview always agree. **PDF limits:** the built-in monospaced fonts only draw English (Latin) letters, so Nepali (Devanagari) text such as a customer's name prints as "?"; dish names on bills are the English ones. **Printer files and the queue:** a web app cannot talk to a printer on your shop's network by itself, so the app keeps a **print queue** and a small program next to the printer collects from it. "Send to printer queue" on an order queues a kitchen slip, receipt or credit note; switching on "Queue a kitchen slip automatically" queues one whenever items are added (one waiting slip per order, listing only items not printed yet, and an item is marked printed only when the printer confirms, so a failed print loses nothing and a reprint never repeats old items). Make a **printer key** under Printing (shown once; only a fingerprint is stored; remaking it stops the old one). The program calls `GET /api/print/agent/jobs` (`Authorization: Bearer pk_...`, optional `?branch=`) to get waiting jobs, each with `escposBase64` (the printer bytes), the same content as `text` lines and the paper width, and `POST /api/print/agent/jobs/<id>` with `{"ok":true}` or `{"ok":false,"error":"..."}` to report. A job tried five times is marked failed (Try again / Cancel on the Printing page). `scripts/print-agent.mjs` is a ready outline for a network printer on raw port 9100. **Not tested on a physical printer:** the PDF and the printer bytes are checked in tests (the PDF structure and byte positions, the ESC/POS commands), but I have not printed on real hardware; expect small adjustments (code page, cutter, port, USB instead of network) on the first real test.

**Owner control** (`/owner`, owner only, with a count of new alerts in the menu). **Daily summary:** sales after refunds, bills, payments by cash, QR and card, refunds, cancelled bills and their value, the cash count at day close (matches, short or over), food cost, best sellers, who worked how long and who was off, and the day's alerts. Switch it on and choose the hour: it is sent once a day after that hour by any of **push** (to the owner's phones that turned alerts on), **text message** (needs a messaging provider on the server, the same as campaign messages) and **email** (needs `EMAIL_PROVIDER` = `resend` with `RESEND_API_KEY` and `EMAIL_FROM`, or `webhook` with `EMAIL_WEBHOOK_URL` and optional `EMAIL_WEBHOOK_TOKEN`). "Send me this now" sends today's on demand, and each channel reports on its own. **Alerts:** checked every 15 minutes while the app runs and shown on the page and by push (between 7am and 10pm): too many cancelled bills in a day, a large value cancelled, a single bill with a large discount percent, discounts that are a big share of the day's sales, a large single refund, and cash at day close that is short or over by more than a limit. Every limit is yours to set (0 switches a check off); an alert is stored once per day and says what was counted and the limit, never who or why. **Bills cannot be deleted:** the app has no way to delete or edit a paid invoice. A mistake is corrected with a credit note (full or part refund) and a cancelled bill keeps its items and its reason; reopening a closed day is owner-only and audited. **For your accountant:** "Download Excel" gives one workbook for a period of up to a year with a daily totals sheet (by branch), every bill, payments by method, refunds with their reasons, cancelled bills with their reasons, and supplier bills (what you owe). Amounts are in rupees; it is a record of what the app holds, not a tax return. The summary and alert checks run from the same 15-minute timer as the automatic campaigns (`AUTOMATION_TIMER=off` disables it; call `GET/POST /api/cron/owner` with `CRON_SECRET` from your own scheduler instead).

**Automatic campaigns** (`/campaigns/automatic`). Two rules, both **off until switched on**: a **birthday offer** (needs the guest's month and day, set on their customer page; no year is kept) and a **win-back** for guests not seen for over 30 days. Once a day per rule, only between 9am and 8pm in the business's own time, the app works out who qualifies: guests who agreed to offers and were confirmed by staff, not messaged in the last 7 days, and not given this kind of message within the rule's own waiting time (300 days for birthdays and 60 for win-back by default; changeable). A rule makes at most one campaign per day (named like "Birthday offer · 2026-11-24") and shows up in Campaigns like any other. Each rule either **prepares** the day's messages for staff to send (any manager can switch that on) or **sends them by itself** through the messaging provider (owner only, and only when a provider is set up; the owner who switched it on is the account the sending runs under, and if they stop being an owner it falls back to preparing). A rule that sends by itself can only be edited by the owner. The same safeguards as manual sending apply: consent re-checked per person at the moment of sending, one message per guest per campaign, daily limit. The timer runs inside the app server (every 15 minutes, started from `src/instrumentation.ts`); set `AUTOMATION_TIMER=off` to disable it and call `GET/POST /api/cron/automations` with `Authorization: Bearer <CRON_SECRET>` from your own scheduler instead. If the app is not running, nothing runs, and a missed day is simply skipped (a birthday message is not sent late: the rule only looks at today). Birthday messages need staff to have entered birthdays; the app does not collect them from guests. Failed automatic sends are retried on each 15-minute check that day.

**Customer OS link** (`/customer-os`, owner only). Connects this app to your Customer OS so it holds your customers and loyalty points. In Customer OS make a key (Settings, Integrations) and paste the address and key here once; the key is checked against Customer OS, stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM, tied to `SESSION_SECRET`) and never shown again. While connected: every paid bill that has a customer is written to a queue in the same transaction as the invoice, then sent to Customer OS as a purchase (the food and drink on the bill, not the delivery fee, at the time of the sale; offline bills too, once they reach the server). Customer OS gives the points by its own loyalty program, and this app's own points are switched off so a bill is never rewarded twice (the local points settings refuse to turn on again until you disconnect). The same bill is never counted twice (Customer OS keys each purchase to the invoice id). If Customer OS is down, the key was revoked or there is no internet, billing carries on, the bills wait and are retried with growing gaps (up to an hour, 40 tries) by the 15-minute timer and on every new sale; a request Customer OS will never accept (for example a sale dated more than 7 days back) is marked failed and can be retried from the page. A customer's points are read back from Customer OS on the order and customer pages (cached for 20 seconds, "unavailable" if it cannot be reached). **Using a reward.** On the order page, once staff have confirmed in person who the customer is, a "Use Customer OS reward" choice lists the customer's earned discount rewards (only ones their points still cover). Taking payment uses the reward in Customer OS first, then pays the bill with the discount. If the bill then cannot be paid, the points are given back straight away, or queued (and retried) if Customer OS cannot be reached. Each use has its own id (the order plus a fresh random part), so retrying a failed bill uses the reward again for real, and a reward that ended up on a paid bill can never be given back later. If Customer OS cannot be reached when the reward is used, the bill is not paid and the cashier can take payment without the reward. A **free-item** reward works too: the cashier picks which item on the bill is free, and one unit of it (at its own price, read from the order, not from the page) comes off the bill; Customer OS does not know which dishes a free item may be, so that is the cashier's judgment. Rewards can only be used online, never offline. **Refunds.** A credit note on a bill that was recorded in Customer OS takes back the points that bill earned, in proportion to the food refunded (the note that finishes the refund takes exactly what is left); points the customer already spent cannot go below zero, so what cannot be taken back is shown on this page as a note on the refund. A refund waits until its sale has reached Customer OS. While connected, a refund no longer changes this app's own points. A refund does not give back a reward that was used on the bill unless the owner ticks "Give back the Customer OS reward used on this bill" on the refund that finishes the whole bill (not on a part refund): the points are credited back in Customer OS (so the customer earns the reward again) and the choice is kept with the bill so it can only be made once. A bill paid before Customer OS was connected has nothing there to take back. A sale made offline and reaching the server more than 7 days later is accepted by Customer OS up to 45 days back (a merchant typing a purchase in there still has 7); a sale older than that is refused and shows as failed here.

**Online ordering** (`/online` for staff, `/o/<token>` for customers). Each branch can take takeaway and delivery orders from a public page the owner shares as a link or a printed QR code. The owner switches it on per branch (pickup and delivery separately), sets a smallest order, the wait customers are told to expect and an optional notice line, and can make a new link at any time (the old one stops working at once). The customer sees the branch's own menu and prices (the same rules as the table QR menu: dishes switched off, hidden or outside the active menu set are not shown), picks dishes and their choices, gives a name and a phone number, and for delivery an address and either a delivery area (when the branch prices by area) or a location (when it prices by distance: coordinates, a map link, or "use my location"). Delivery is priced by exactly the same code as staff-made delivery orders. **Payment is cash (or QR or card, if the restaurant takes it) at pickup or at the door: there is no online payment yet.** An order is only a *request* until staff accept it: it appears under Online orders (with a badge in the menu, a push notification and an instant refresh), and Accept turns it into a normal takeaway or delivery order in one transaction (the dishes and choices are checked and priced again at that moment; if something ran out the accept is refused and the request stays waiting; two staff accepting at once make one order). Reject takes a reason the customer sees. The customer follows their order on a status page (waiting, accepted, being prepared, ready, completed; for delivery a link to the live tracking page) and can cancel until it is accepted. Requests nobody answers for an hour expire (checked by the 15-minute timer) and the customer is told. Because nobody is signed in on the public page: prices, choices and fees are always worked out on the server; one phone may have at most 2 requests waiting and one branch 40; the public actions are rate limited per address; and the customer's phone number becomes a customer (for loyalty) only when staff accept, so spam never fills the customer list. The number is unconfirmed (typed by the customer), so staff confirm it in person before points can be spent. Not built: opening hours (staff switch it off by hand), online payment, Nepali text on the checkout form (the menu itself already switches language), and telling the customer by SMS.

**Guest Wi-Fi** (Branches page, per branch). The owner enters the guest network's name and password (and whether it is WPA, old WEP, open, or hidden); a blank name switches it off. The table QR page then shows a "Free Wi-Fi" card, before the guest starts ordering and during their sitting: the network name and password with copy buttons (in English or Nepali), and a "connect another phone" QR code in the standard Wi-Fi format (`WIFI:T:WPA;S:...;P:...;;`, with the special characters escaped) that a phone camera turns into a join offer. **A web page cannot join a Wi-Fi network by itself (no phone allows it)**, so the guest still copies the password and picks the network in their phone's settings; the QR code is for joining a second device, and for the printable poster. The Tables, QR codes page prints a "Free Wi-Fi, scan to connect" poster for the current branch, with the name and password in words under the code. The limits are the real Wi-Fi limits (name at most 32 bytes, WPA password 8 to 63 characters, or 64 hex digits). The password is shown to anyone who holds a table's QR code (like a sign on the wall), so use a guest network, not your main one; it is never written to the audit log.

**Install as an app (iPhone, Android and desktop, no app store).** The staff app and each restaurant's customer app are installable web apps. *Staff:* signing in shows an "Install the staff app" card in the side menu (and on the rider page); it opens full screen from the home screen, with shortcuts for Orders, Kitchen and Quick order when you long-press the icon on Android, and on iPhone it is what makes background alerts possible. *Customers:* the online ordering page and the order status page show "Add to your home screen" (in English or Nepali); the installed app is named after the restaurant, has its own icon (the first letter of the restaurant's name) and opens that restaurant's ordering page. On Android and desktop Chrome or Edge it is one button (the browser's own install dialog). **iPhone and iPad have no install button for web pages**, so the card shows the two taps to make by hand (Share, then Add to Home Screen). A card the person closes stays away for 14 days. The app's service worker keeps nothing private: only a plain "You are offline" page (shown instead of the browser's error when a page cannot be reached) and the app's own script files, plus the Quick order page for offline billing. **Phones only offer to install, and only allow the service worker and alerts, on a secure address (https; plain `http://192.168...` over Wi-Fi is not enough, localhost is fine on the computer itself)**, so test installing on a phone through an https link (a tunnel, or your real hosting). Real App Store and Google Play listings are a separate step (wrapping this same web app in a thin native shell, which needs an Apple Developer account and a Mac for iOS) and are not done.

**Floor map and phone order-taking (staff).** The Orders page opens with the floor drawn as a map: a tab for each area, and each table drawn with its chairs (a round table for up to 6 seats, a long table with chairs along both sides for bigger ones; the seat count is the table's capacity, set on the Tables page). A free table is grey; a table with an open order is teal with a tick, its running total and item count; a table that needs someone (a guest's order waiting, a call for the waiter, water or the bill) wears an amber ring and a bell. Tap a free table to start its order, or a busy one to open it. Takeaway and delivery have their own small form below the map. On the order page, dishes are picked from a list built for a thumb: search, category tabs, a photo (or a coloured letter tile) for each dish, big + and - buttons, and a bar at the bottom ("3 items selected, Rs 590") that opens the list to review and sends everything with one tap. Nothing reloads while picking (before, every dish sent the page back to the top). Dishes with a required choice open a sheet to choose. The list is added all or nothing in one transaction, and the kitchen gets one slip for the whole list. Not built: a note for each dish from this list (use the old note by editing the order), and dragging tables to match the real room (tables are laid out in rows by area).

Still not built: roads drawn on the map or turn-by-turn navigation, a true shortest-route solver, automatic scheduling (the alert check needs something to call it), push on iPhone without installing the app (an Apple rule), payroll tax calculations, paying suppliers or staff from inside the app, and receiving customer replies (STOP) by message: opt-outs are recorded by staff.

Multi-branch note: guests' QR codes follow their table's branch.

## Hosting it on your own network

For using it on the shop's Wi-Fi (tablets, phones, guests scanning table QR codes) from this computer:

```bash
npm run db:dev                      # the database; leave it running in its own terminal
npm run build                       # once, and again after any code change
npx next start -H 0.0.0.0 -p 3000   # the app, reachable by other devices on the network
```

- Set `APP_URL` in `.env` to the address devices will use, for example `http://192.168.1.66:3000` (this computer's address on the Wi-Fi; `ipconfig` shows it). QR codes and share links are built from it. If the router gives this computer a different address later, update `APP_URL`, rebuild is not needed: restart the app. Reserve the address in the router to avoid that.
- Sign-in and guest cookies are only marked Secure when `APP_URL` starts with https, so plain-http addresses on a local network work.
- Windows may ask to allow Node.js through the firewall on the first start; allow it for the private network.
- Background push alerts and installing the app on iPhones need https (a real domain with a certificate); on plain http the in-app sound and pop-ups still work, but alerts when the app is closed do not.
- The embedded database allows one connection at a time, so the integration tests cannot run while the app is running. Stop the app first.

## Accounting books (Nepal)

The **Accounts** page (owner and manager can see it, only the owner can change it) keeps double-entry books: a chart of accounts for a restaurant, the journal, the daybook, the ledger of any account with a running balance, the trial balance, profit and loss, and the balance sheet. Every paid bill, refund, supplier bill and supplier payment is posted by itself, once (the journal's source key is unique), so the books follow the till. Dates show the Nepali (Bikram Sambat) date beside the English one, and vouchers are numbered per fiscal year (1 Shrawan to the end of Ashadh, like "2083/84") with no gaps. Vouchers are never edited or deleted: a mistake is put right with a reversing entry. Payment, receipt, contra and journal vouchers can be typed by the owner. A voided supplier bill is reversed in the books automatically.

Not built yet: the IRD billing rules (fiscal-year invoice numbers, buyer PAN and the BS date on the bill, "copy of original" on reprints, sales and purchase registers, VAT return, CBMS link), input VAT on purchases, TDS, payroll posting, bank reconciliation, opening balances, year-end close, and CSV/PDF export of the books. The IRD page linked in the original request has no software requirements on it; these are taken from the Electronic Billing Procedure 2074 as described by secondary sources and must be checked against the official text and IRD's own approval process before any claim of approval is made.
